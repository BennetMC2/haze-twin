      How the twin works

      1. Every six hours the twin takes in Singapore's PSI and PM2.5 from NEA, fire hotspots from NASA's VIIRS satellites, and the NOAA GEFS weather ensemble (31 forecasts).
      2. For each weather forecast it releases particles at Singapore and traces them back up to four days, through that forecast's winds and rain. Where the air passed over fires, it picks up smoke.
      3. On top of each forecast it samples what is uncertain: how much smoke the fires give off, how long they keep burning, how much rain puts them out, new fires, and background pollution. 100,000 futures in all.
      4. Futures are weighted by how well they match what NEA measured over the last day, so the twin starts from the real air.
      5. Peat fires only dim under steady rain (more than about 6 mm a day), and part of the haze is regional smoke that lingers whatever the wind does.
      6. The further ahead, the more the futures lean on how similar days in past seasons (2015, 2019, 2023 and this year) actually played out, using NEA's PSI history since 2014.
      7. News reports from the last three days are sorted into signals (fires spreading, emergencies, firefighting, rain, easing). The balance nudges fire growth in the futures by at most 2% a day, and the page shows by how much.
      8. Futures are grouped into storylines by outcome. The odds are the share of futures in each. Rerun repeats the sampling on your device with NEA's latest reading.
      9. Every forecast is kept as issued. Two days later it is scored against what NEA measured, misses included.

      Three horizons

      • Days 1–6: the full twin. Storylines and daily odds come from here.
      • Days 7–15: the same twin on the longer weather forecasts. Wind forecasts lose skill after about a week, so the odds widen and the page shows chance by date rather than a middle line.
      • Season outlook: when rain sets in over the Kalimantan and Sumatra fire areas. Taken from the weather forecasts where they reach it; beyond that, from normal onset dates shifted later by El Niño and a positive Indian Ocean Dipole. Experimental.

      Measured

      • NEA 24-hr PSI, 24-hr and 1-hr PM2.5 (data.gov.sg)
      • NASA FIRMS hotspots, VIIRS on NOAA-20 and NOAA-21
      • Airport visibility near the fires (METAR)
      • Regional PM2.5 stations (OpenAQ)
      • Recent rain over the fire areas (Open-Meteo)
      • NEA PSI history since 2014 (data.gov.sg)
      • Satellite true-colour imagery (NASA GIBS, NOAA-20)
      • El Niño (NOAA RONI) and Indian Ocean Dipole (NOAA DMI)

      Simulated

      • Winds and rain from 31 weather forecasts: NOAA GEFS to 16 days and ECMWF IFS to 10 days when available
      • Back-traced air paths to Singapore
      • Fire smoke, storylines and odds
      • CAMS smoke model, shown for comparison

      News headlines come from GDELT and link to the original reports.

      Limits: smoke amounts are scaled to match NEA, not measured at the fires. The PSI conversion uses NEA's PM2.5 breakpoints. This is an independent public-good project; for health advice, follow NEA.
