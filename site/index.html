      How it works

      Haze Twin is a computer copy of the region's fires, smoke and winds. It plays out 100,000 possible next two weeks and counts how they turn out.

      1. Every six hours it takes in Singapore's PSI from NEA, fire hotspots seen by NASA satellites, and 31 weather forecasts from NOAA.
      2. For each weather forecast it traces the air arriving in Singapore back up to four days. Where that air passed over fires, it picks up smoke.
      3. It then varies what nobody knows for sure: how much smoke the fires give off, how long they keep burning, how much rain dims them, and new fires.
      4. Futures that fit what NEA measured over the last day count for more, so the forecast starts from the real air.
      5. Further ahead, it leans more on how similar days in past haze seasons (since 2014) actually played out.
      6. News from the last three days nudges fire growth slightly, by at most 2% a day.
      7. The odds on the page are the share of futures with each outcome. Rerun repeats this on your device with NEA's latest reading.
      8. Every forecast is kept. Two days later it is scored against what NEA measured, misses included.

      How far ahead

      • Next 6 days: the full model. The outcomes and odds come from here.
      • Days 7 to 15: the same model on longer weather forecasts, which lose skill after about a week, so the ranges get wider.
      • End of season: when steady rain sets in over the fire areas, from the weather forecasts and, beyond them, from normal dates adjusted for El Niño. Experimental.

      Measured

      • NEA PSI and PM2.5 (data.gov.sg)
      • NASA FIRMS fire hotspots (VIIRS)
      • Airport visibility near the fires
      • Regional PM2.5 stations (OpenAQ)
      • Recent rain over the fire areas (Open-Meteo)
      • NEA PSI history since 2014
      • Satellite imagery (NASA GIBS)
      • El Niño and Indian Ocean Dipole (NOAA)

      Simulated

      • Winds and rain from 31 NOAA GEFS forecasts
      • Air paths traced back from Singapore
      • Smoke, outcomes and odds
      • CAMS smoke model, for comparison

      Limits: smoke amounts are scaled to match NEA, not measured at the fires. This is an independent public-good project. For health advice, follow NEA.
